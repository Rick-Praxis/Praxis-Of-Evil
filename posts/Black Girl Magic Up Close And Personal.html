Black Girl Magic Up Close And Personal

Blog · September 2026

    

The other day I got out of work and drove to the bar a couple blocks down from the shop. I parked my car and then walked to the party store next door, picked up a pack of smokes to enjoy between beers and walked out. Outside there was a young black woman, figured she was in her 20s, standing near the other end of the building. She was blathering in her people’s special ghettoized vernacular to no one in particular when I made the mistake of making eye contact with her. She started saying things to me but I couldn’t comprehend them as is so often the case when people like this attempt to engage with me. I put my head down, turned, and began walking away towards the bar entrance.

Years of living in Detroit conditioned me to never respond to anyone like this, there was no good that would come out of it. I walked past my car and contemplated getting inside and driving off to spare me any extended interaction with this woman. But I figured fuck it, people like her usually post up in a single spot and don’t typically deviate from it, or so I thought. And anyway I was thirsty and didn’t feel like dealing with downtown traffic to get a beer on the main drag. I entered the bar, took my usual seat and ordered my usual drink, my attention was divided between my phone and the sports highlights playing on the TV.

I was checking Twitter on my phone when I heard an all too familiar voice behind me, I turned to see the parking lot girl and she took the stool next to me holding a water she’d gotten from the barmaid. I froze up and instinctively put my phone in my pocket. She was saying something to me but her voice didn’t rise above a mutter. She had this look in her eyes similar to what I’d seen in those videos of black women who began trashing liquor stores after the clerk doesn’t refill their EBT cards. I tensed up, not wanting to take my eyes off of her but not wanting to give her the time of day either. She didn’t look disheveled or homeless, part of me wondered if black girls are just like this all the time.

A moment later the barmaid arrived and the tension broke momentarily, she told the black girl that she couldn’t just loiter and actually had to buy something if she wanted to stay. I guess she’d been in the bar the night before asking people to buy her drinks an the management was none too keen about it. It turned into an argument because of course it did, you can’t reason with people like this because they’re incapable of understanding that what they’re doing is wrong. They’ll just loiter or trespass until the cops have to get called because they feel entitled to do whatever the fuck they want 24/7. I could sense the tension rising again so I noped my White ass out of there, walked outside and had a smoke knowing full well this girl wasn’t going to be reasoned with.

The door was open and after a moment I could hear the black girl yelling at the top of her voice about how the barmaid, a nice lady named Shayna, was a “bitch ass hoe” along with other insults that you can imagine if you’ve ever seen a video from inside a Waffle House. To tell a black woman she’s unwelcome is the ultimate insult and the consequences will be threats, phone recording or, as is so often the case, physical violence. I didn’t want to be in a TikTok with a racism hashtag so I bided my time and finished my smoke. The yelling had stopped and I walked back in to see the barmaid and the other patrons sharing a laugh. A cop was near the entrance and Shayna told him that the girl had just stormed out, he nodded and left to go schizo hunting.

I walked back to my seat and saw there was a black guy sitting at the bar with an embarassed look on his face, he’d probably seen displays like this all throughout his life and felt ashamed about it just as much as the white trash bullshit I’d seen made me feel ashamed. As I finished my drink I heard the other patrons, mostly middle aged white guys, remarking about how this sort of thing just doesn’t happen around here. And they were right, my city is majority white, my neighbors were mostly white, the establishments you go into are staffed by white people and have white customers. You just don’t see this sort of thing outside of the aforementioned videos from less than stellar businesses in or around the hood.

I don’t want to be in the hood though, I don’t want it creeping into my new neighborhood. I lived in the Detroit suburbs for 4 years and managed not getting fucked with because I avoided people like that black girl like the plague the entire time I was down there. I don’t want fucked up hood rats bothering me when all I want is to get a nice buzz going after work. But this is just what you have to deal with when living in proximity to these people, and they know they can usually get away with it too. If you’re white and make the mistake of confronting the wrong black person it can end very badly for you.

The girl had apparently been recording on her phone and screaming that Shayna and everyone else in the bar had been racist which made my decision to avoid the situation entirely a smart one. I’m not really a luddite but I’m well aware of how the onset of technology empowers people like this, there’s a whole online demographic of people who get stirred up over any example of racism regardless of how legitimate it is. I’d wager the vast majority of “racist” businesses or people you see being put on blast by women like this are totally fabricated. They likely only made the single but deadly mistake of telling a black woman no.

If you’re white and have the misfortune of interacting with women like this you’re essentially in a hostage situation, parking lot girl held all the cards, the internet would take her side if someone stepped in and tried to remove her and she’d be justified in doing whatever damage she wanted to do to the establishment. At some point in recent history we decided that racism was the worst possible crime of all and as such removed all agency from retarded black women. There’s this image in popular media of black women and how special and important they are and I know it’s unfair to categorize all black women as being insane as parking lot girl but the way we pretend women like her don’t make all our lives miserable is infuriating.
