THE SHOP


Dan Liman stood at the hydraulic press watching as the main cylinder forced the 4-ton punch down onto the steel blank with 1500 tons of pressure. There was the sound of the steel being forced into shape between the punch and die, it sounded like metal crumpling during a car accident. After a couple of seconds, the pressure released with a loud swish and with the flick of a switch Dan brought the main side back up with a loud groan from the cylinder. He engaged the safety and walked up to the die to see a perfectly formed quarter panel waiting to be pried out of the press and put on a pallet. He was about to grab the pry bar off his toolbox when there was a voice behind him.


“Dan!”


He jumped and wheeled around to see Old George, the semi-retired team lead sitting on a forklift. George apologized for scaring him then told him the general manager Alan needed to see him, Dan asked what for and the old man shrugged,


“Hell if I know, but he was pissed…”


Dan shrugged then smirked when he saw Old George was on his trusty Komatsu, he was the only one in the shop who could get the thing running. He playfully asked George how he did it, the old man smiled at him,


“You just gotta show her a little affection!”


He patted the side of the forklift then drove the it and the pallet of cold rolled steel it was carrying towards the main floor. He left Dan to ponder what he could have done to earn the GM’s ire that day. His boss’s boss was a prick, a doughy balding boomer who micromanaged the hell out of everyone and would fly into a rage over piddling bullshit. Dan avoided him as best he could which wasn’t difficult since Alan was posted up in his office the majority of the time arguing with suppliers on the phone or watching videos on his computer with the volume turned all the way up. That was typical for United Tooling, the machine shop Dan had worked at for the better part of a decade.


He could sometimes hear Alan screaming at some poor guy in his office from all the way across the shop, which was impressive considering how loud the machinery was. Dan sighed, slipped off his gloves and tossed them on the bed of the press then retrieved his Marlboros from his toolbox and slid them into his pocket. He had the feeling he’d deserve a smoke break after listening to Alan’s bullshit.


Making his way across the shop towards the office Dan saw Fitzy sitting at the Press Brake bending brackets into shape, his stringy gray hair running down his neck from under his ball cap. Josh, the young guy, was at the smaller 800 ton press working on rocker panels and old George was sitting on an idling forklift shooting the shit with Patrick, a heavy set former Marine who’d lost two fingers of his right hand on a lathe. He would joke that he never got shot during his two tours in Afghanistan and only got battle scars at his 9-5. He spotted Dan and laughed, shouting


“You’re in for it now bud haha!”


All the guys at the shop had been dressed down at one point or another by Alan. The asshole had chased more than a few guys off with his dogshit personality. Dan used to work with a guy named Tim who ran the laser and one afternoon Alan had started screaming at him like he fucked his wife. Tim had grabbed the fat bastard by the collar, and it took Dan, Josh, and George to pull the two apart. Tim quit on the spot and walked out, and it had bummed Dan out because the guy had jokes and was always one to bum you a cigarette. Some days Dan wished they’d let Tim, a well-muscled guy with at least 4 inches on Alan, crack the fat prick in the jaw.


Dan made his way into the office and could already hear Alan yelling from down the hall, his current victim was Christine, the receptionist who’d apparently failed to send an important email to a supplier on time. He was a boomer but he knew enough about technology to make their lives a living hell via email and text whether they were in or out of the shop, nowhere was safe from him if you had a device with a WiFi connection. Alan was blathering about personal responsibility when Dan walked up to the doorway to see Christine with her head down absorbing the verbal abuse. He could smell the trademark scent of his boss, Old Spice spray masking body odor. Alan finished his rant, Christine turned and she and Dan’s eyes met for a half second, the look she gave him said Be careful. Dan got sad thinking about Christine, when she started a couple of years ago she had been a cheerful, bubbly person but Alan had stolen the light from her eyes. Dan had once asked her why she didn’t just quit and she’d told him she had two kids and a felony on her record and this company was the only one who’d hire her. Dan could sympathize, he’d been in the clink for a while himself, he’d knocked over a few liquor stores along with some B&E when he was a kid, he told himself he stole to survive but he’d be lying if he said he didn’t get a thrill out of it. A lot of the guys he worked with over the years had similar backgrounds to his and to Alan’s credit he still brought them on. Christine disappeared down the hallway and Dan closed the door and turned his attention to his boss,


“What’s up Alan?”


He honestly couldn’t think of what the big man would be upset about, so much so he needed to be dressed down, but it wasn’t like he hadn’t gotten his tit in the wringer for inconsequential shit in the past, Alan was extremely petty. His boss eyed him cooly, two beady eyes squinting behind wire glasses on a fat, red face.


“You know exactly what’s up.”


Alan growled, Dan hated playing these games, internally he said Just give me the spiel and let me get back out on the floor you fat fuck. Alan continued,


“Last Wednesday you had two hours of downtime on the 1000 ton and didn’t log it Liman, where the fuck am I supposed to get those two hours from? Your paycheck?”

It clicked for Dan, he’d had to drain the oil from the press and take the motor apart to install a new gasket. It had been sitting unused for days despite him filing a maintenance request and leaving several notes that his boss, of course, ignored. A customer had a large order for specialty parts and Dan couldn’t wait. Alan always insisted on calling his guys for repairs because he didn’t trust his own employees, it didn’t matter that Dan had 14 years of experience working and maintaining presses. All in all, he figured he’d saved the company a few grand, but Alan didn’t give a shit about that. All that mattered was that he didn’t file some meaningless paperwork. Alan continued, gesticulating his pale, flabby arms. His bingo wings shook, Dan used every bit of his concentration not cracking a smile,


“You know damn well that we have a specialist for taking care of the presses, it’s a waste of time for you to climb up there and start fucking around, you could have broken something and then we’d be fucked, or don’t you care?”


Dan offered an apology but, as per usual, Alan didn’t want to hear it and preferred to keep bitching. He had a half-eaten double bacon cheeseburger sitting on greasy paper in front of him, next to it was a bottle of his heart medication. Alan would go on about how expensive his medical bills were, but he still insisted gorging on every bit of cheese covered slop he could get his chubby hands on, Dan remembered his uncle eating himself into an early grave. Alan went on for a while, small flecks of food flying out of his gaping mouth as he rambled about responsibility and communication.

Dan let his mind wander, at this point Alan’s yelling just sounded like echolalic babbling to him, just noise for the sake of noise. Then Alan mentioned docking his pay and Dan snapped out of it,


“What do you mean you’re docking me?”


He wasn’t one to rock the boat; Alan had threatened to fire him before and he knew how shitty the job market was currently. He’d figured that over the years he could take some shit every once in a while if it meant a steady paycheck, but this was bullshit. Dan explained as calmly as he could that he’d informed Alan about the issues with the press weeks before he repaired it. His boss wasn’t hearing it,


“You wasted two hours of company time!”


Alan insisted while Dan’s anger began welling up,

“I needed to get those parts done! You were complaining about the late orders over the last few months and said we couldn’t afford any more mistakes. Hell, I probably saved the company thousands!”


For a brief moment there was a look of shock on Alan’s doughy face, the normally timid Dan had finally found his balls. Then the boss’s eyes narrowed and his face grew a deeper shade of red, no one talks to him that way,


“You listen to me Liman, you follow the fucking rules, you’ve been here long enough to fucking know better!”


Dan clenched his fists but held his tongue, the thought of unemployment on his mind. He wasn’t one to question capitalism but the amount of shit he put up with for a paycheck weighed on his mind. A smirk appeared on his bosses face,


“Hell, you were the one who probably fucked the press up in the first place you dick head!”


Dan was normally quiet and reserved, he’d taken his share of abuse and let it run off of him, but not today.

“Fuck you Alan, you fat piece of shit! I bust my fucking ass and all you do is sit and stuff your fat face! You never did a hard day’s work in your life!”

He would brag about how much smarter he was than his superiors at the other companies but couldn’t land a stable job until daddy intervened, the guy was still a manchild into his 50s and had a chip on his shoulder to the point he’d chase off any semi-competent worker like he was exacting revenge on his old bosses.

“Get the fuck out of here you cocksucker! You’re fucking fired! I can get 5 Mexicans to do the job for half your paycheck and I…”

It slipped out of his grasp and fell to the floor, rolling to Dan’s feet. Alan staggered towards the bottle on unsteady legs, his face was a shade of red that Dan didn’t think was humanly possible. The boss fell to his knees a few feet away from him and began reaching for the bottle. Dan thought for a second while his boss made guttural moans then kicked the bottle under the shelf against the wall next to him. Alan looked at him with a mixture of fear and rage, his teeth clenched with spittle running down his chin and his hand clawing at his chest. Dan would never forget that gaze as his boss’s heart finally gave out and he fell flat on his face mere feet from where he was standing.

Dan stood there in shocked silence looking at the dead white blob on the floor that had been his boss. His immediate thought was that it was over, no more yelling or screaming or bullshit, no more forced overtime or docked paychecks, no more walking on

eggshells.

There came a knock at the door behind him, it was Christine asking if everything was okay, once again Dan snapped out of his stupor, opened the door and told Christine to call 911. He saw Christine’s eyes dart to Alan’s corpse and for a microsecond her expression looked to him like she was thinking Oh God please let this bastard finally be dead. She turned away and pulled out her phone.



Roughly 30 minutes later the United Tooling crew watched the coroner drape a sheet over the bloated body of their now former boss and wheel him into a meat wagon. Nobody knew what to say, even Patrick, who normally never shut up, just stood there with a shocked expression, Dan couldn’t remember the last time the guy was at a loss for words. Lost in thought he didn’t hear Old George ask him how he was, the old man walked up and touched his arm, startling him.


“You alright bud? That must have been scary.”


Dan could only summon a mutter in response, then a moment later he found his voice,


“I’m fine George, I just uhhhhh….I don’t know what to think right now.”


He was telling the truth; he was wrestling with what he did or didn’t do rather. But if he was really being honest he just felt numb. Alan’s beet red face would be on his mind for a long time. The old man looked sympathetic and patted him on the back while Fitzy lifted his tattered ball cap with one hand and smoothed back his long gray hair with the other,


“Well, I guess that’s that…”


He pulled out a Maverick and popped it between his lips. Josh looked confused, perhaps even a little saddened, he hadn’t been at United long enough to build up the animosity the other’s had for Alan. At least that’s what Dan figured as the kid asked,


“Should we uhhh…should we say something? I mean the guy just died.”


That stung Dan as he thought of himself as somewhat responsible for Alan’s death, or had he been? He figured it was only a matter of time before the pudgy bastard had the big one, every time he saw his boss he’d either been screaming or eating, a heart attack was overdue. Dan figured he’d just helped him along. Old George put a hand on Josh’s shoulder,


“We can say a quick prayer for him if you’d like.”


Josh nodded and Patrick scoffed, Old George shot the ex-marine a look that disarmed him and Fitzy rolled his eyes,


“If I’m gonna pray it will be to thank the good lord for putting him out of our misery.”

“Did you get in touch with Halloran?”


Christine nodded,


“Yeah, he told us to take the rest of the week off while he sorts this out, he’s probably going to bring in a higher up from the plant in Brighton, at least temporarily. He gave me a number for a counselor in case any of us have grief or whatever.”

Dan figured Bob liked Alan because he handled the dirty work, but now he himself would have to get involved. It’s not every day your corporate attack dog dies of a coronary.

Fitzy flicked away his cigarette,


“I don’t know about you guys but I’m gonna head to Sully’s, first round’s on me!”


Everyone except Old George the Teetotaler exclaimed, Josh said he could go for a cold one and Patrick jokingly asked if he was even old enough to drink which made everyone laugh. They headed back into the building to shut down and lock up. Dan powered down his press and began gathering his things, his favorite sounds were that of heavy equipment being powered down. He walked back up to the front office and saw Christine at her desk, she turned to him,


“Hey could you shut off the lights and lock up the offices? I just have to send a few emails really quick.”


Dan told her it wasn’t a problem and made his way down the hall flicking switches and turning locks until he got to Alan’s office. Some of the furniture had gotten moved around by the paramedics as they muscled his considerable heft onto the stretcher and wheeled him out. He saw that Alan’s burger was still on the desk and he didn’t want to leave it sitting for the rest of the week, he figured he could at least put it in a bag and toss it into the dumpster. The group laughed and Dan forced a smile; he still had Alan’s swollen face and terrified expression on his mind.


His boss’s death was something he’d never forget, he had technically killed him or at least sped the process along as he’d thought earlier when they were wheeling Alan’s body away. He was a few beers deep, but he didn’t feel any regret, Alan had made all of their lives miserable for years and no one, including that dick Halloran, had done anything about it. The way he figured nothing of value was lost, he had done the company a favor and hoped whoever replaced Alan was less of a douchebag.

Patrick was sharing stories now, he recalled the time the office toilet had broken and Alan, who’d had a burrito from a questionable Mexican place for lunch, wound up shitting his pants. That was the rumor anyway, Christine said it definitely smelled like the boss had messed himself and remembered him tying his jacket around his waist and leaving early that day, the table shared a laugh and Dan excused himself to head out for a smoke.


In the alleyway behind the bar Dan leaned against the graffiti and sticker strewn brick wall while lightning up a cigarette. There was a group of 20 somethings standing roughly ten feet away, a tall curly haired guy and two pretty young women on either side of him, watching what was probably a Tiktok on his phone and laughing. There was another guy closer to Dan’s age on the other side of the alley wearing a suit and arguing with someone on the phone, maybe his wife, maybe his side piece. Dan smiled and took another puff as the rear door opened and Christine appeared, she asked for a cigarette which Dan happily provided. He watched her light it and asked,


“How are you holding up, Chris?”

“I…I’m a little fucked up to be honest, I mean I hated that bastard but…this.”

“I figured this day would come eventually, I mean the guy was so unhealthy and he was always so fucking mad, he was due to go out this way, surprised it didn’t happen sooner.”


Christine took a long drag of her borrowed cigarette then exhaled and looked up at the sky with a thoughtful expression, Dan wondered what she was thinking about then she turned to him,


“Alan assaulted me you know…”

But he had noticed a change in Christine since she’d been working there, she seemed like such a sweet person he couldn’t imagine anyone trying to take advantage of her, but Alan was a special case he supposed.
